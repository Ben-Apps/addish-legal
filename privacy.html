Privacy Policy

Addish app (iOS and Android) · Last updated: September 24, 2026

The short version
  • No sign-up, no e-mail, no password. To use the online features, the app creates an anonymous guest ID for you with our hosting provider. It contains no name or contact details.
  • No ads, no tracking across apps or websites, no analytics or crash-reporting tools. We don't sell your data.
  • Your profile, check-ins, saved plates, pantry and notes are stored only on your phone.
  • When you use a photo scan, the menu helper, a pantry photo or the craving helper, and when the app suggests adds for a plate, what you give it (photo or text) and some context (for example your wishes and food preferences) goes to our server and on to an AI service (Google Gemini, with Anthropic as backup). We don't store your photos or request texts.
  • Our server keeps a few records linked to your guest ID: free-try counts, usage records and, if you send it, your feedback. They are pseudonymous, not anonymous.
  • Subscriptions run through Apple or Google and RevenueCat. We never see your payment details.
  • “Delete all my data” in the app wipes your phone. To have the records on our server deleted, write to ben@creaiter.com (details in section 9).

2. What stays on your phone

Addish stores the following only on your device (in the app's local storage). We cannot see it:

  • your setup: wishes, food preferences (for example vegetarian, halal or gluten-free), tone, gentle mode, situations, reminder time, language;
  • your hunger check-ins and the patterns written from them, the words you pick for them, and your answers to the short question after a meal;
  • saved plates, your plate history, your pantry list, what you liked or passed on (“What I remember”), leftover notes and similar notes;
  • the sign-in token for your guest ID (see 3.1), a small counter for free tries, cached app settings, and unsent feedback waiting for a connection.

Reminders (the daily nudge, a leftover reminder you asked for, and one reminder before a free trial ends, if notifications are allowed) are scheduled on your phone. There is no push server.

Delete it: Settings → “Delete all my data” erases all of this from your device and removes the local link to your guest ID. It does not cancel your subscription, and it does not delete the records on our server described in section 3. For those, see section 9.

3. What leaves your phone

3.1 Your guest ID (anonymous sign-in)

The first time you use an online feature, the app signs in anonymously with Supabase Auth, our hosting provider's sign-in service. This creates a guest user with a random ID (a UUID) and a session token, which the app keeps on your phone and refreshes when needed. You don't enter a name, e-mail address or password. Every request to our server carries this token; our server checks it and uses the verified guest ID to check your subscription and count free tries. The same guest ID is used as your customer ID at RevenueCat (3.8).

3.2 Photo scan (plate)

When you scan a plate, the app sends to our server:

  • the photo, shrunk to at most 1024 pixels on the long edge. Before sending, the app removes embedded photo metadata such as location, time and device;
  • the photo's width and height (used only to estimate processing cost);
  • context that makes the suggestions fit you: your wishes, food preferences, tone, gentle mode, where you are (for example “restaurant”), the rough time of day and the app language.

Our server forwards the photo and the context to an AI provider (see 3.7) to recognize the food. The answer comes back to your phone, where you can correct it. The app reads the captured photo and then deletes the file; the full-size original is never uploaded.

3.3 Add-on suggestions for a plate

After you confirm a plate (scanned or typed), the app can ask our server for fitting add-on ideas automatically. It sends: the foods on the plate (IDs and names from the app's food list, and names you typed), which ideas you already accepted or have seen, a short list of candidate foods, meal or snack, whether you are about to eat, the situation, whether you want no-cook ideas, optional texture, cuisine or craving hints, and the context from 3.2. It can also include your pantry foods, the adds you called tasty or not your thing, and how hungry you said you are. Food preferences can include allergy-related choices such as egg-free, soy-free or sesame-free. Only foods the app's list knows are passed on to the AI provider; your own dish names and free craving text are not.

3.4 Menu helper

If you paste menu text (up to 4,000 characters) or take a photo of a menu, the text or photo is sent with the context from 3.2 to our server and on to the AI provider, to suggest easy adds. A menu photo is shrunk and stripped of metadata like a plate photo.

3.5 Pantry photo

If you fill your pantry from a photo (a receipt, your fridge, a shelf or a single food), the photo, the kind of photo and the context from 3.2 go to our server and the AI provider, which lists the foods it recognizes. Nothing is added to your pantry until you confirm it, and your pantry list stays on your phone.

3.6 Craving helper

If you type a craving the app doesn't know yet (up to 200 characters, plus optional tags such as “now” or “tired”), the text and the context from 3.2 go to our server and the AI provider.

3.7 AI providers

Our server sends the content from 3.2–3.6 to Google Gemini API (Google LLC) first. If Gemini fails or is unavailable, the same request goes to Anthropic (Anthropic PBC, Claude models) as a backup. We use the paid API tiers of these providers, under which they do not use API content to train their models. They may keep inputs and outputs for a limited time under their API terms, for example to detect abuse. We do not use any other AI provider; before that changes, we will update this policy.

We do not store your photos or request texts. Our server does not save or log photos, menu text, craving text or the request context. What it does store is described in 3.9.

3.8 Subscriptions and premium check

Purchases are made through the Apple App Store or Google Play. They process your payment as independent controllers under their own privacy policies; we never receive your payment details. RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.) manages your subscription status. The RevenueCat software in the app and our server share with RevenueCat your guest ID as customer ID, your purchase and subscription information (product, dates, status, store receipt) and technical device information the RevenueCat software collects by default (such as device model, operating system version and app version). Our server asks RevenueCat whether your subscription is active and remembers the answer for 10 minutes in memory. If the ID is new to RevenueCat, RevenueCat creates a customer record for it.

3.9 What our server stores

Our database stores three kinds of records. They contain no name, e-mail address, photos or request texts, but they are linked to your guest ID: either the ID itself or a keyed hash of it (HMAC), which stays the same for the same guest ID. They are therefore pseudonymous, not anonymous.

  • Free tries: your guest ID, the feature (plate scan, menu helper, pantry photo) and the time of each free try, so free tries can't be used twice.
  • Usage records: for each AI request, a request number, the feature, the time, the AI provider and model, the result (success or error), error codes, processing time, amount of data processed, cost, whether it was a free try or premium, and the hash of your guest ID. We also count requests per day under the same hash, for daily limits. We use this to run the service, fix errors, control costs and prevent abuse.
  • Feedback: only if you send it — your note (up to 1,000 characters), thumbs up or down, the screen it came from, the app version, the platform (iOS/Android), the app language, a random submission ID and, only if you tick the box, the foods on the plate you were looking at (never a photo). Before storing, our server removes e-mail addresses and phone numbers from your note and stores it with the hash of your guest ID. A few notes per hour and day are accepted. To understand what people ask for, we later have stored notes grouped and summarized by the AI providers from 3.7.

3.10 Hosting and server logs

Our server and database run on Supabase (Supabase, Inc.) in the AWS region us-east-1 (USA). When your phone connects, the hosting infrastructure processes your IP address and technical connection data (time, requested address, device software) to deliver and secure the service. Our own function logs contain only technical metadata (feature, status, provider, processing time), never photos, texts or user IDs.

3.11 App settings from our server

The app occasionally loads a small public settings file from our server (for example how many free tries there are). This request carries no user ID.

3.12 Fonts, links and this website

The app's fonts are built into the app; showing them sends nothing to anyone. Links to helplines, this policy or the terms open in your browser or phone app only when you tap them; the linked services have their own privacy policies. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). When you open it, GitHub receives your IP address and processes it in its server logs to deliver and secure the site. The website uses no cookies, scripts or external fonts.

3.2 Foto-Scan (Teller)

Wenn du einen Teller scannst, schickt die App an unseren Server:

  • Breite und Höhe des Fotos (nur zur Schätzung der Verarbeitungskosten);
  • Kontext, damit die Vorschläge zu dir passen: deine Wünsche, Essensvorlieben, Ton, sanfter Modus, wo du bist (z. B. „Restaurant"), die ungefähre Tageszeit und die App-Sprache.

Unser Server leitet Foto und Kontext an einen KI-Anbieter weiter (siehe 3.7), der das Essen erkennt. Die Antwort geht an dein Handy zurück, wo du sie korrigieren kannst. Die App liest das aufgenommene Foto ein und löscht die Datei danach; das Original in voller Größe wird nie hochgeladen.

3.3 Extra-Vorschläge für einen Teller

Nachdem du einen Teller bestätigt hast (gescannt oder eingetippt), kann die App unseren Server automatisch nach passenden Extras fragen. Um zu verstehen, was sich Menschen wünschen, lassen wir gespeicherte Notizen später von den KI-Anbietern aus 3.7 gruppieren und zusammenfassen.
